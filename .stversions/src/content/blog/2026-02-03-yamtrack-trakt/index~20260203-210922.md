About a year ago I [made a video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ht75bGtniyc) about some dreadful decisions being made by the people behind Trakt, which until then had been my favourite movie and TV show tracking website for several years.

Well, it seems like bad decision has followed bad decision and now even more users, alienated by Trakt's latest updates, are searching for alternative platforms.

---

## Trakt

It is a shame, really. I discovered Trakt around 2012, and even [wrote a blog post about it](https://thomid.me/review-of-trakttv/) around that time, describing it as "a magnificent website which I highly recommend to lovers of TV or film." For many years it was a pretty and easy to use website that made it easy as pie to track the media you watch. And in my opinion it blew competitors like Letterboxd out of the water by tracking TV shows as well as movies.

It also had a great community feel. Big enough to get clever little comments on most episodes of popular shows, but small enough that you'd often see the same commenters and get to know their tastes. There are a handful of other users I genuinely enjoyed following, always interested in what their take would be on the latest episode of Dexter or The Good Wife. Some of my own little one-line reviews were even pretty popular:

![](Pasted%20image%2020260203170205.png)

Trakt always had some interesting VIP features behind a paywall, but they were never related to the main tracking or list-making features. VIP was affordable, too. At $15 USD a year, it was early on that I thought, "hey, I like this site, I'm happy to throw in a few bucks!"

Cut to now, and I've been a Trakt VIP member for 13 years. But that subscription is ending in a few weeks, because this is no longer the friendly little tracking website it used to be. Basic features like making long lists of shows or movies are now partially locked behind a paywall. And VIP subscriptions have blown up to $60 USD a year — for everyone, no grandfathered rates.

Alongside this, the developers of the site and app seem determined to continually try to fix what ain't broke. 

Trakt Lite is the latest version of the site, that's been available in preview for some time. When it was first announced, I was one of many who asked "what is this and why are you making it", and never received a coherent response. Now, it seems they're gearing up to make this "Trakt Lite" version of the website the main one. And people aren't happy.

Why? Because it's simply worse. It's harder to navigate. It has missing features. It obscures access to simple navigation and tracking. The whole thing looks more like a mobile app than a website. And that is the only positive thing I've heard anyone say about it — it runs fast and it's usable on mobile. But it's not what anyone wants.

![](Pasted%20image%2020260203201857.png)

These changes go hand in hand with a mobile app redesign that came out of the blue and completely changed a platform that many people loved. The pushback against these changes on Reddit and Trakt's forums over the past few weeks has been extreme.

![](Pasted%20image%2020260203204026.png)

![](Screenshot%202026-02-03%20201120.png)

Possibly most egregious of all is the partnership with Younify to facilitate automatic scrobbling from streaming services. A feature that sounds pretty cool and useful on paper but is rumoured to be one of the key reasons they now need to charge $60/year. This demonstrates Trakt's core mission change from friendly community website to expensive commercial platform.

I have nothing against automatic scrobbling features, but if that's what they cost to run for each user, something is rotten. I just want a basic and easy-to-use website that remembers everything I've watched. Ultimately this is a glorified spreadsheet to me, with pretty pictures. But Trakt now seems to think it's some kind of fancy premium media kiosk.

I'm don't know the numbers, but my guess would be that Trakt is probably actually pretty popular. Perhaps it has more users (and more paying users) right now than it ever has. Perhaps many of them are even pleased with the latest changes. But just because something is mainstream, doesn't mean it's not stupid. And Trakt had something unique, which it now does not.

Some users will hate these changes, but stick it out with Trakt. And sure, the higher costs and worsening interfaces can be tolerated... but on principle I do not want to be a part of a userbase that is so obscenely and consistently ignored by its custodians.

Hence, the search for a new tracker.

There are actually a lot of alternatives out there, like [Simkl](https://simkl.com/) or [Serializd](https://www.serializd.com/) (why are the names of all apps are just letter salads nowadays?).

But I've been burned enough. Sure, I could try my luck with another closed source web service from developers I don't know with unclear monetisation plans... or I could break free, and use something open source.
## Yamtrack

![](Pasted%20image%2020260203173132.png)

[Yamtrack](https://github.com/FuzzyGrim/Yamtrack) is a self-hosted open source media tracking app that began development just a few years ago.

Yep, you've gotta self-host it. I swear that's not as daunting as it may sound, if you've never self-hosted before. But I get it, it can be hard to get started. Since I've been self-hosting for a couple years now, it's a topic I'm keen to write about much more soon.

But being free and open source are huge boons here. My data isn't trapped in some service with a tenuous future, it's mine. Each update and change is transparent and it's my choice to accept it. The worst possible outcome for a project like this is that it's abandoned by the developer. Certainly a potential scenario, but one that still leaves me in full control of all of my data and plenty of options.

![](Pasted%20image%2020260203173231.png)

And Yamtrack is just what I want. It's like the Trakt of yore: just the basics without too many frills. It's easy to find, 

Yamtrack can even track other media like books and video games. I haven't tried these yet but I don't see why they shouldn't work quite well. Transferring in my book history from StoryGraph is on my to-do list for some point in the future.


Of course, Yamtrack isn't perfect yet. It's still so new, how could it be? These are some of the most important features I'm patiently awaiting:

- Rating and commenting on individual episodes of TV Shows
- Easier controls for dropping and rewatching TV Shows
- Wrapped/Year in Review end of year watching recaps

One day it's easy to imagine (much less easy to implement, I'm sure) a federation feature that allows all Yamtrack servers to share comments between one another. At that point we essentially return to feature parity with Trakt at its best, the difference being we have total control of our data.

I do believe the internet can actually be that good. One step at a time!
